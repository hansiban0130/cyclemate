1 · The health problem

Infertility affects about 1 in 6 adults worldwide (WHO, 2023). IVF is effective — but only when a patient completes the cycle.

The gap: up to 1 in 5 patients drop out of IVF before finishing, and the leading reason is not medical failure. It is the burden of treatment itself. Most who stop never return.

The IVF journey, in one line. Baseline scan → 8–12 days of stimulation injections, monitored by scans and blood tests → trigger shot → egg retrieval → fertilisation in the lab → embryo transfer → two-week wait → pregnancy test. About 28 days; most of it spent at home between visits.

Why a leaflet is not enough. IVF is not one protocol. During stimulation the dose and timing of each injection are adjusted to the patient's own hormone response, scan by scan — the plan for day 9 is only known on day 8. Every patient therefore walks a different, moving schedule, and no clinic can re-explain it by phone to every patient every day. The gap between "what the doctor decided" and "what I'm supposed to do tomorrow" is where confusion, anxiety and drop-out begin.

What "burden" actually is

From clinic experience in Tashkent and the literature, four things wear patients down:

🌫️Uncertainty — "what happens next?"
📋Daily load — visits, pills, timing
🕯️Feeling alone between visits
❓Nowhere to ask a small question

Target users

  • Patients going through an IVF cycle — primary user. Many live far from Tashkent and cannot visit daily.
  • Clinic coordinators — see who needs a call first, instead of waiting for patients to reach out.
  • Doctors — receive only the questions that need a medical answer.

Meet Oy — why a moon

A lunar month and an IVF cycle are both about 28 days. Oy waxes through stimulation, is full at embryo transfer, and wanes toward the pregnancy test. One glance at the moon tells a patient where she is — no calendar, no reading.

Calm by design. Medical apps often use alerts, red badges and countdowns that raise anxiety. Oy replaces them with a soft, familiar companion who reacts to how the patient feels — so the app lowers fear instead of adding to it.

A moon, not an embryo. Oy stands for the cycle, not the outcome. Patients are never asked to bond with an embryo; if a cycle fails, the moon simply begins again.

A word for today. Under the task list sits one of 28 short lines, one per cycle day — e.g. day 7: "You are doing better than you think you are." They never promise an outcome; they only keep the patient company.

3 · Privacy, security & ethics

Privacy & securityLogin only with the phone number registered at the clinic (SMS code). Data encrypted in transit and at rest, stored on the clinic's own server. Patient chooses what to share — partner sees schedule only, never mood or messages. Deleted 12 months after the cycle, or on request.
EthicsAI gives general information only; medical questions go to a human. When unsure, it forwards rather than guesses. Flags prioritise a coordinator's calls — they never label or diagnose a patient. The companion is designed to reduce fear, not to build attachment to an outcome.

Equity & access

  • Web-based, no app store — runs on any low-end phone; reminders fall back to SMS when offline.
  • Built for patients outside Tashkent — only 4 visits are essential; remote check-ins and partner clinics cover the rest.
  • EN / UZ / RU — icons and one task per screen for low-literacy users.
